The story opens with a signature line: sign here to agree to the Terms of Service. Signing without reading is the point. Four funny clauses are hidden in the fine print for you to find, and there is a short game where you spot the catch in three made-up clauses. Most of us agree to far more fine print than we ever read. Researchers McDonald and Cranor estimated in 2008 that reading every privacy policy you run into would take about 244 hours a year, roughly a month of full-time work.

Then the camera flies through the fine print to three clauses from a sample lease. Real leases vary, and local rules may limit clauses like these.

Clause 4: Unless either party delivers written notice of non-renewal not less than sixty (60) days prior to expiration, this Lease shall automatically renew for a successive term of twelve (12) months at a rental rate determined by Landlord in its sole discretion. In plain English: If you forget to say no 60 days before it ends, your lease renews for another year, and your landlord picks the new rent.

Clause 6: Landlord may deduct from the deposit any amounts necessary to remedy damage beyond ordinary wear and tear, as well as costs of cleaning, repainting, and carpet replacement, regardless of the condition of such items at move-out. In plain English: They can bill you for new carpet and paint even if the old ones were already worn out.

Clause 7: Landlord and its agents may enter the Premises at any time for the purposes of inspection, repair, or showing the unit to prospective tenants, with or without notice. In plain English: Your landlord can walk in whenever they like, without warning you first.
